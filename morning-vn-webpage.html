A NOTE BEFORE YOU BEGIN
What follows is not invented. It is my own account of something that happened to me, described as precisely as memory allows. No names have been changed, no events dramatized beyond what I actually experienced — the words are my own, written the way I would tell it to someone sitting across from me.
WRITTEN & ILLUSTRATED BY MAHFUZUR RAHMAN
THE ROOM, BEFORE ANYTHING HAPPENED
It was still dark, the particular dark just before dawn admits it's losing. The room looked exactly as it always does at that hour — quiet, familiar, unremarkable. I want you to hold that image, because it matters later: nothing about that room, in that moment, suggested anything was wrong.
SOMEWHERE BETWEEN SLEEP AND WAKING
I woke because something was hitting my back. Not fully awake — that strange middle place where the room exists but the mind hasn't agreed to return to it yet.

Something was moving violently behind me.
HER HAND
It was my wife's hand. Jerking back and forth, striking hard enough to wake me. A nightmare, I thought. Maybe a seizure. Maybe apnea.

I turned toward her. I reached for her hand and grabbed it, trying to steady the movement. For a moment, I thought that was all I needed to do.
TWO OR THREE SECONDS LATER
Within two or three seconds, her entire body began shaking.

Violently.

By then I was fully awake. The last remnants of sleep disappeared almost instantly. I looked at her face.

And that was when everything became impossible.
HER FACE, BEGINNING TO CHANGE
Her skin seemed to be melting away. Not metaphorically. Not the way a nightmare distorts things — I could see it happening, in real time, the way wax gives way under heat. Her features began to lose their human structure, as though the skin itself were dissolving and pulling back from whatever was underneath.
WHAT THE SKIN LEFT BEHIND
I don't say this lightly, and I've thought hard about whether to describe it at all: what came through was not raw or bloody in the way an injury would be. It was drier than that. Tissue drawn back like old parchment, tightening against bone that had no business being visible. The more I looked, the worse it became.
HER EYES
Her eyes had sunk deep into their sockets — not closed, not rolled back, just gone further into her skull than eyes should be able to go, two points of faint, wrong light sitting in the dark where they should have been.
HER MOUTH
There were no lips left to make a mouth out of. What remained was only the architecture underneath — a jaw, and teeth, set in an expression no face is meant to hold while breathing beside you.
WHAT WAS LEFT WHERE HER FACE HAD BEEN
What was lying beside me, for those seconds, had no face left to recognize as hers. A skull. Her skull, I have to assume, though nothing in me wanted to make that connection at the time.

For some reason, I didn't scream. I didn't even let go of her hand. I froze. Completely. My body simply stopped answering to me.
UNABLE TO MOVE
I could see what was happening. I could understand that it was horrifying. But I couldn't move. I couldn't pull my hand away. I couldn't turn my head. I couldn't even seem to breathe properly.

I just watched.
THE SOUND
Then she began to speak.

There were no lips to form the words. I could hear the teeth grinding against each other as she spoke. A harsh, unmistakable sound.

grinding — speaking — grinding —

I don't remember exactly what was said. What I remember is the sound.
A STRANGE CALM
Strangely, somewhere in those seconds, my fear disappeared. It didn't gradually fade — it was simply gone. I became completely calm.

I started thinking instead. But I didn't feel asleep. My eyes were wide open. Everything looked real. Too real. Except for her.
THE VEIL
A thin veil seemed to surround the bed. Not a curtain. Not smoke. Not fog — something thin and almost transparent, as though an invisible layer had been drawn around us, and the space inside it had become separate from the rest of the room.

I noticed it, but by then I wasn't particularly frightened. I was thinking. If this was some kind of entity, what did it want?
A PRESENCE, JUST OUTSIDE THE VEIL
I want to be honest about something I've hesitated to include: I had the distinct, unshakeable sense that we were not alone in that space the veil had made. Nothing I could point to — no shape I could swear to in a court of law. Just the certainty, the way you know someone is standing behind you before you turn around.
TWENTY, MAYBE TWENTY-FIVE SECONDS
I finally forced myself to move. I sat up.

And the instant I did, everything stopped. The skull was gone. The dissolved skin was gone. The sunken eyes, the teeth, the veil, the presence I couldn't name, the strange lilac light — all of it, gone.

My wife lay beside me exactly as she should have been. Peacefully asleep. Completely, ordinarily normal.
A FEW MINUTES LATER
I lay there facing away from her, trying to calm myself, trying to convince myself to go back to sleep. Maybe I had dreamed it. Maybe I hadn't.

Then something hit my back again. Her hand. The exact same movement.

Fuck. No. Not again.
"WHAT HAPPENED?"
This time I didn't grab her hand. I pushed her shoulder, trying to wake her normally. She stirred. Her eyes opened. Half asleep.

"What happened?" she asked.

I didn't answer. She got up, went to the bathroom, came back, and went to sleep again. No skull. No dissolved skin. No grinding teeth. No veil. No presence. Nothing. Just my wife, sleeping beside me.
I still don't know what happened that morning. I don't believe it was supernatural. But I can't honestly say I understand it.

I know what I saw. I know what I felt. And I know that for roughly twenty seconds, maybe a little longer, I watched the woman sleeping beside me become something that looked nothing like a human being.

Then I sat up. And it was gone.

It's the fact that, for those few seconds, I was completely convinced that something impossible was happening right beside me.

And then reality simply switched itself back on.
